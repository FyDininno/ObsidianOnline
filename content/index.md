# Node Form

> The "Node Form" project was an open sourced plugin made for Blender. It is an extension that allows users to enter a mathematical parameterization into a graphical interface and receive an animated visualization of their transformation. 

![[SineForm.gif|500]]
![[spherical_transformation.png]]
![[shellgrow2.gif]]

Mathematical transformations are quite interesting, but not everyone has the background to visualize them from looking at equations alone. This Blender plugin gives anyone the ability to visualize mathematical parameterizations. During my freshman year of college, I finished my first version of the program. It was not until nearly a year later when I completely finalized this project. The results of which can be found here on my [github](https://github.com/FyDininno/NodeForm), and the tutorial for this plugin is [[Tutorial|linked here]].


# Graphene Composite Compression Machine
> The following graphene compression machine is meant to handle one thousand pounds of force and is machined from stainless steel. It was a central device to synthesize our team's desired thermal composite material.

![[Pasted image 20251231232426.png|300]]![[Pasted image 20260610154846.png|400]]
![[Pasted image 20260610154905.png|300]]![[Pasted image 20260610155055.png|300]]
![[photo_2026-06-10_15-58-03.jpg|500]]

The goal of our team's senior capstone project was to study the thermal properties of compact graphene composites. My role in the project was to model and machine the prototype which is shown above. Under the application of 1000 pounds of force distributed over a 50mm^2 crossection, we were able to compress low-density graphene powder composites into compact, pi-stacked solids. The project lasted six months, involving both wet chemistry and mechanical processing, and thermal conductivity increases were observed in graphene composites which possessed a high concentration of diamond-intercalated microparticles.

# Alameda Garden

> The Alameda Garden is a specialized garden bed designed for the residents of the Alameda Homeless Shelter in San Jose. It is rollable, elevated, and is able to be closed and locked during the night.

![[final_garden.png|350]] ![[Pasted image 20260610164544.png|350]]
![[Pasted image 20260610164447.png]]

I attained the rank of Eagle Scout on November 9, 2021. My Eagle Project was to build a garden bed for the residents of the Alameda Homeless Shelter. Of the activities available to them during the quarintine times, gardening was one that many enjoyed. However, due to security restrictions, there were no garden beds available to them, only pottery. This project provided the residents with a garden bed which remained secure during the night. 
For more details on the project, information can be found [[Eagle Project|here]].

# Nanosecond-Switching Langmuir Gates
> The following is a theoretical device proposed as a component of a larger genetic printing chip. The proposal has been submitted to the Nano-Innovator-Grant for evaluation for Summer 2027.

![[Pasted image 20260610164934.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260610164834.png]]

This work was the result of one summer's worth research in the field of biophotonics at UCSD. One of the central goals of the research was to discover a way to synthesize DNA strands longer than the current upper limit. It was theorized that a molecular gate which could switch its gating state with near-perfect efficiency within a few microseconds could help achieve this goal. However, no solid-state nanopore in the literature met this criteria. The gate proposed here, if functional, is believed to fulfill this criteria. The full documentation for my proposal may be found [here](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ME5CjU42ZG51NW1HRjlz_1ZrXsjiaVNf/view?usp=sharing).
